# 5. Privacy and Confidentiality

The researcher will make reasonable efforts to protect the confidentiality of the farmer and farm.

When possible, identifying information such as:

- Farmer name
- Farm name
- Farm address
- Exact geographic coordinates
- Contact information
- Other identifying information

will be removed, masked, or replaced with a unique identifier before data is used for research or shared with other researchers.

Results will generally be reported in an aggregated form so that individual farms cannot easily be identified.

However, the farmer understands that complete anonymity may not always be possible, particularly when datasets contain unique characteristics or when information is combined with publicly available information.

---

# 6. Data Security

The researcher will use reasonable technical and organizational measures to protect the data.

These measures may include:

- Password-protected systems
- Encrypted storage and data transfer
- Access controls
- Limited access to authorized researchers
- Regular data backups
- Secure data storage
- Removal of unnecessary identifying information

If a data breach occurs that may significantly affect the farmer or their data, the researcher will follow applicable institutional and legal requirements for notifying affected parties.

# 7. FAIR Data Principles

The research team will manage the data according to the **FAIR principles**:

## 7.1 Findable

Research datasets and outputs should be organized so that they can be discovered and understood by appropriate users.

Where appropriate:

- Datasets will be assigned persistent identifiers.
- Data will include descriptive metadata.
- Metadata will describe the dataset's source, collection methods, variables, and time period.
- Research outputs may be registered in appropriate research repositories.

Identifying information about individual farms will not be made publicly findable unless the farmer has explicitly agreed to such disclosure.

---

## 7.2 Accessible

Research data should be accessible to appropriate users while protecting farmer privacy and confidential information.

Where appropriate:

- Researchers may access de-identified datasets through a research repository.
- Access restrictions may be placed on sensitive datasets.
- Metadata may remain publicly accessible even when the underlying data cannot be publicly downloaded.
- Requests for restricted data may require approval from the research team or data steward.

Public access to data does not override privacy, confidentiality, legal, or contractual requirements.

---

## 7.3 Interoperable

Data should be stored and documented in formats that allow it to work with other research systems and datasets.

Where practical:

- Common file formats such as CSV will be used.
- Standard units of measurement will be documented.
- Variable names and definitions will be clearly described.
- Data dictionaries will be provided when appropriate.
- Standard agricultural terminology or controlled vocabularies may be used.
- Dates, measurements, and other variables will follow documented standards.

Any conversions or transformations made to the original data will be documented.

---

## 7.4 Reusable

Data should be documented well enough that it can be appropriately reused for future research.

Where appropriate:

- Metadata will describe how the data was collected.
- Data processing steps will be documented.
- Data quality limitations will be identified.
- The version of the dataset will be recorded.
- Appropriate licenses or terms of use will be included.
- Citations will be provided for the dataset and associated research.

Future reuse of identifiable or confidential farm data will require appropriate permission and will follow the terms of this agreement.
